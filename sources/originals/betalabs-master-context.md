## What They Do (One Sentence)

BETA Labs books confirmed pre-event 1:1 meetings between event sponsors and their ideal customer profiles — enriching raw attendee lists with external data, then proactively qualifying and scheduling meetings before the conference starts.

---

## Business Model

**Performance-based, event-linked, managed service (not SaaS). Dual-sided: two distinct customer types, two distinct sales motions.**

### The Two Customer Types

| Customer Type | Role | Value Proposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Customer — Event Organizer** | Essential partner + distribution channel | "We unlock the hidden value in your attendee list, help you retain sponsors by guaranteeing their ROI, and create a new revenue stream you can offer at zero development cost." |
| **Beneficiary Customer — Sponsor/Exhibitor** | End-user + paying client | "We deliver pre-qualified, confirmed meetings with high-intent ICPs, saving you time and ensuring you generate real pipeline from your event investment." |

> **Organizers are both a customer (BETA Labs sells/pitches to them) and a distribution channel (they introduce BETA Labs to their sponsor list).** Sponsors are the paying client — they pay per qualified meeting delivered.

### How the Service Works

1. BETA Labs signs a partnership agreement with an event organizer
2. Organizer sends an introduction to their sponsor list, or includes BETA Labs in a high-tier sponsorship package
3. Interested sponsor defines their ICP with BETA Labs
4. BETA Labs receives the raw attendee list (under NDA), **enriches it with external data sources**, and identifies high-intent attendees matching the sponsor's ICP
5. BETA Labs proactively contacts, qualifies, and books confirmed 1:1 meetings — pre-event
6. Sponsor shows up with meetings already on the calendar

**No technical integration required from the organizer — just the attendee list.**

### Pricing Models

| Model | Structure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard (direct/outbound)** | $100/qualified meeting, performance-based | Sponsor pays only for confirmed meetings held |
| **Reseller (via organizer)** | Fixed package (e.g., $1,500) sold by organizer, with ~15% revenue share to organizer | Most common: 8 of 11 clients at $1,500 |
| **Bundled** | Included in organizer's high-tier sponsorship package ($10K–$15K+) | Increases perceived value of Platinum tier; organizer earns on total package |

**Minimum floor: $750 (10 matches). Standard: $1,500 (15 matches). Premium standalone (CDWare): $4,000 (15+30 matches).**

### Three Purchase Motions

1. **Organizer-introduced (primary):** Organizer emails their sponsor list → sponsor books a meeting → closes in 1–15 days. Fastest, most scalable.
2. **Direct outbound (secondary):** Founder or team emails companies attending a specific upcoming event → closes in 1–10 days. Requires advance knowledge of the event's sponsor/attendee list.
3. **Walk the Floor / standalone (emerging):** No specific event — BETA Labs does always-on ICP matchmaking. Higher ACV ($4,000) but slower cycle (21–78 days). Only confirmed example: CDWare.

## Core Strategic Pillar: The Event Catalogue

BETA Labs' growth strategy is built around proactively securing a portfolio of recurring event partnerships — the **"Event Catalogue"** — before pursuing sponsor acquisition.

**Logic:** Events are the "building shell." Sponsors are the "tenants." Without events in the catalogue, there is no inventory to sell sponsors. The catalogue creates a compounding flywheel: more events → more sponsor opportunities → more proof points → easier to sign more events.

**Planned execution:**
- Proactive outreach to high-quality events (1,000+ attendees, tech/B2B SaaS/MarTech verticals)
- Website feature planned: public-facing page listing all partner events → inbound lead magnet for sponsors browsing upcoming events
- Partnership Guide ("Canal 3") in development: a playbook enabling organizers to act as effective resellers, spot opportunities, handle objections, and sell BETA Labs to their own sponsors without hand-holding

## Sales Process

### Motion 1 — Acquiring Event Partners (Building the Catalogue)

Goal: sign recurring event organizers as distribution partners.

| Stage | Step | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Outreach** | Identify target events | 1,000+ attendees; tech, B2B SaaS, MarTech verticals; strong sponsor/exhibitor revenue; annual/recurring format |
| | Initial pitch | "We unlock hidden ROI in your attendee list. Your sponsors get guaranteed pipeline. You get a new revenue stream and higher retention." |
| **Closing** | Partnership structure | Define model: revenue share (~15%), package inclusion, or bundled tier |
| | Contract signed | Formalize terms + NDA for attendee list sharing |
| **Enablement** | Onboarding + Partnership Guide | Provide the "Canal 3" playbook — how to spot opportunities, handle objections, and sell BETA Labs to sponsors without BETA Labs in the room |

### Motion 2 — Acquiring Sponsors (Filling the Catalogue)

**Timing sweet spot: ~1 month before the event.** This is when urgency is real but there's still time to execute the matchmaking.

**Deal velocity KPIs:**

| Performance Tier | Time: First Contact → Deposit |
|---|---|
| Exceptional (anomaly) | 1–2 business days |
| **Ideal benchmark** | **5–7 business days** |
| Sub-optimal | 10+ business days |

**Typical full cycle (organizer-introduced):**

```
Organizer sends email to sponsor list (Day 0)
→ Sponsor fills interest form or books meeting (Day 0–1)
→ Intro call + ICP alignment (Day 1–5)
→ Proposal sent (same day as call)
→ YES decision (same day or next day)
→ Contract + invoice sent (within hours)
→ Contract signed + 50% deposit (within hours to 1 day)
→ ICP finalization call/emails (Days 1–5)
→ Deliverable: confirmed meetings booked (Day 5–12 pre-event)
→ Final payment (on or after event)
```

**Fastest deal on record:** Mirego — intro call, YES, contract, deposit all on 5 novembre 2025 (same day). 1 day from first email to deposit.

**Slowest viable deal:** CDWare — 21 days to GO, 78 days to deposit. Walk the Floor motion, no event urgency. Still worth pursuing for ACV.

**Deals to avoid:** EY — 162 days to GO, no deposit after 5+ months. Enterprise procurement destroys the model's urgency advantage.

**Pilot project precedent:** Expedibox negotiated a pilot (10 matches at $750). Acceptable floor — frame as "pay only for confirmed meetings, zero risk." Never discount below $750.

## ICP Sweet Spot

### For Organizer Acquisition (Motion 1)

**Recurring B2B conference or trade summit held in a convention center, 1,000+ attendees, with significant sponsorship/exhibitor revenue and cross-industry attendance — where sponsor ROI and retention are active pain points for the organizer.**

Buyer: Event Director, Sponsorship/Partnership Manager, CEO of event company.

**Refined selection criteria (updated March 2026):**

| Criterion | Requirement | Disqualifier |
|---|---|---|
| **Venue environment** | Convention center (Palais des congrès, convention halls, trade show floors) | Hotel ballrooms, hotel meeting rooms — **hard disqualifier** |
| **Attendee volume** | 1,000+ registered participants | Under 1,000 — pipeline too thin for matchmaking density |
| **Industry mix** | Prioritize heterogeneous events: tech/B2B SaaS vendors selling *to* less-tech buyers (retail, manufacturing, construction) | Pure tech-only or pure vertical-only events (lower cross-sell density) |
| **Format** | Annual/recurring (repeatable revenue + compounding proof points) | One-off or inaugural events only |
| **Attendee composition** | Cross-industry (buyers from multiple sectors present) | Supplier/vendor-dominated events (AWS Summit pattern) — too promotional, poor cross-industry buy-side density |
| **Pain signal** | Organizer struggling with sponsor retention or sponsor ROI perception | Organizer satisfied with Grip/Swapcard output — no active pain |

**Why heterogeneity matters:** The highest-value matchmaking scenarios occur when a tech vendor needs to reach buyers from a less-tech industry (e.g., a MarTech company selling to retailers, or a Construction SaaS selling to general contractors). These buyers don't self-select into "meetings" on event apps — they need proactive qualification. HOP! Le Sommet du commerce de détail (Mirego → retail context) and Grand Batimatech (Autodesk/Premier → construction buyers) are the template.

**Why convention center matters:** Convention center environments have structured floor plans, dedicated booth spaces, and a physical layout designed for business exchanges. Hotel events are typically smaller, more informal, and rarely feature the sponsor density or cross-industry attendance that makes matchmaking economically viable for BETA Labs.

### For Sponsor Acquisition (Motion 2)

**B2B SaaS or Marketing/Tech services company, 20–200 employees, confirmed sponsor or exhibitor at a specific upcoming Quebec or Ontario B2B conference within 2–8 weeks, with a GM, VP Marketing, BDM, or CEO as the direct buyer — motivated to generate qualified pipeline, not brand visibility.**

The single most important qualifier: **a specific upcoming event creates the urgency that makes this product sell itself.** No event context = 3–5x longer cycle with no natural close date.

## ICP Tier Definitions (Sponsor Motion)

### Tier 1 — Ideal
- **Company:** B2B SaaS or B2B services, 20–200 employees
- **Industry:** Marketing, MarTech, Sales Tech, B2B Software, Digital Agency, Tech Services
- **Buyer:** GM, VP Marketing, BDM, or CEO with direct budget authority (no committee)
- **Situation:** Confirmed sponsor or exhibitor at a specific upcoming B2B conference in Quebec or Ontario
- **Time to event:** 2–8 weeks (urgency window is active)
- **Pain:** Has attended events without a pre-meeting strategy; measuring ROI by badge scans or booth traffic
- **Source:** Organizer-introduced OR direct outbound with event-specific hook

*Real examples: Mirego, Cyberimpact, Digital Revamp*

### Tier 2 — Strong
- **Company:** B2B SaaS or professional services, 150–400 employees
- **Industry:** Construction Tech, Retail, Finance, or other B2B sectors
- **Buyer:** Marketing Director, BDM, Partnership Manager, or Sales Director
- **Situation:** Sponsor/exhibitor at an upcoming event OR Walk the Floor with explicit pipeline objective
- **Time to event:** Up to 12 weeks
- **Pain:** Does multiple events per year, can't attribute pipeline to them

*Real examples: Expedibox, Nexus, Autodesk (organizer-introduced), CDWare (Walk the Floor)*

### Tier 3 — Qualify Carefully
- Enterprise (400+ employees): expect 3–6 month cycle. Only pursue via established organizer relationship.
- Government: Avoid direct outbound. Only through organizer partner.
- No confirmed upcoming event: Lower priority. Flag for next event season.
- "Event" title only (no GM/VP/BDM): Engage to qualify, require an economic buyer intro before advancing.

*Real examples: EY, Gouvernement du Québec*

## Competitive Landscape

BETA Labs does not compete with event apps — it layers on top of them and enriches what they can't do.

| Competitor | Who they sell to | Limitation vs. BETA Labs |
|---|---|---|
| Grip (2.6/5 App Store) | Event organizers | Optimized for organizer metrics (check-ins, engagement); basic industry-correlation matching ("Excel formulas") |
| Swapcard | Event organizers | Attendee self-selection; no proactive outreach or external data enrichment |
| Brella | Event organizers | Attendee-initiated matching, not sponsor-driven |
| EventMobi | Event organizers | Full event management platform; meeting quality is secondary |

**The core differentiator vs. Swapcard/Grip:** Those platforms match on raw attendee list data (simple industry tags). BETA Labs **enriches** the raw attendee list with external sources before scoring — identifying true purchase intent and ICP fit, not just industry overlap. The output is a qualified, human-confirmed meeting — not an algorithmic suggestion the attendee may never act on.

**Adjacent alternatives (sponsor-side DIY):**
- LinkedIn Sales Navigator + manual outreach before events
- Internal SDR doing pre-event prospecting
- Event organizer's internal concierge (for top-tier sponsors at large events)

**BETA Labs' core positioning argument:** "The organizer's app is optimized for their metrics, not your pipeline. We work for you — and we start with richer data than they have."

## Positioning and Messaging

**For event organizers (Motion 1 pitch):**
> "Add a 'Premium Qualified Leads' package to your top sponsor tier. We do all the work, you earn ~15% of every package sold. Your sponsors get a tangible ROI — confirmed meetings, not badge scans — and they renew next year."

**For sponsors (Motion 2 pitch):**
> "You're spending $10K–$50K on this event. Let us pre-book 15 confirmed meetings with your exact ICP before you arrive. You pay $100/meeting — only for meetings that happen."

**Key objections and responses:**
- *"The organizer's app already does matchmaking"* → Their app matches on industry tags — our version of Excel formulas. We enrich the list with external data to find actual purchase intent, then we proactively contact and confirm the meeting. It's not the same thing.
- *"We don't have bandwidth for this"* → That's exactly why we exist. You define your ICP once in a 20-minute call. We handle everything else. You just show up.
- *"$100/meeting seems expensive"* → Average B2B lead costs $200–$400 with no guarantee of a meeting. We deliver a confirmed, calendar-blocked meeting with a qualified ICP. One closed deal pays for the entire package 10x over.
- *"What about Loi 25?"* → All attendee data is handled under NDA, used exclusively for this event's matchmaking, and deleted post-delivery. We can share our data handling policy on request.
- *"We already have a booth/app from the organizer"* → We're not a replacement — we're an add-on. Your team still works the booth. We just make sure there are already 15 pre-qualified people booked to come see you.

## Key Differentiators

1. **Data enrichment layer:** Raw attendee list + external data sources = true ICP intent scoring, not industry tag matching
2. **Pre-event, not during-event:** Meetings are on the calendar before the event starts — no chasing, no wasted floor time
3. **Performance-based pricing:** Pay per confirmed meeting. No results = no charge. Zero financial risk.
4. **ICP-defined, human-confirmed:** Meetings filtered against the sponsor's exact criteria, then personally confirmed with each attendee
5. **No tech integration:** Organizer shares attendee list. No IT project, no procurement delay.
6. **Additive to existing event tools:** Works alongside Grip/Swapcard/EventMobi. Not a replacement — a complement.

## Strategic Priorities

1. **Build the Event Catalogue first.** Target 8–12 recurring event partnerships in Quebec + Ontario before scaling sponsor outbound. Events are the distribution channel — without them, the sponsor motion has no anchor. Prioritize: **convention center venue**, 1,000+ attendees, **cross-industry attendance** (tech vendors + non-tech buyers), recurring annual format. **Filter out hotel events at sourcing stage** — they fail the venue and density criteria.

2. **Launch the public Event Catalogue page.** A website page listing all partner events acts as a passive inbound lead magnet for sponsors browsing upcoming events — lower CAC, higher intent.

3. **Develop and distribute the Partnership Guide ("Canal 3").** Enable organizers to sell BETA Labs to their own sponsors without founder involvement. This is the leverage point that lets the channel scale without proportional headcount.

4. **Protect the $1,500 price point.** 8 of 11 clients paid it. Anchor every conversation to the ROI math — one closed deal from a qualified meeting pays for the package many times over.

5. **Turn Cyberimpact into a case study.** First repeat client (Social Next Toronto) = the clearest proof that the model works and creates loyalty. A published case study with before/after metrics unlocks credibility for both organizer and sponsor pitches.

6. **Deepen the Marketing vertical.** Cyberimpact, Axeptio, Digital Revamp (clients) + Glowtify, HighLevel (prospects) = natural concentration. A Marketing-specific outreach sequence ("for MarTech sponsors who attend tech events") will outperform generic messaging.

7. **Develop a Loi 25 one-pager.** Proactively share it with any prospect over 100 employees. Eliminates a procurement stall before it starts.

8. **Explore CDWare motion deliberately.** Test "always-on matchmaking" with 2–3 more companies. If it closes at $4,000 consistently, it becomes a distinct product line with a separate ICP and sales motion.

9. **Track internationalization signals.** Canada/North America is the current focus. Dubai/Middle East is a named future target — culturally distinct sales motion required. No action yet, but note for strategic planning.
