## How to use this file

Every email, deck, post, one-pager and call script assembles from the pieces below — nobody writes a pain point or an objection answer from scratch again.
Framing (anchor, differentiators, vocabulary, claims) is **not** changed here; that goes through `/positioning`.
If an artifact needs a piece that isn't here, add it here first. If it needs a number that isn't cleared, get it cleared — don't write around the freeze.

**Allow-list (usable numbers):** 62% exhibitor retention / 38% churn (cite CEIR) · 11 paying clients across 6 events · 1 repeat client · the HOP! organizer quote · $100 per match delivered · 15–25 matches · delivery one week before the event.
**Deny-list:** any booking/scheduling of meetings · "no results = no charge / zero risk" · "30% of first-time attendees return" · "AI-driven/AI-powered" (🟡) · MeePayGo 45% and 80% (🟡) · 35–45% conversion (🟡) · 100% ICP alignment (🟡) · attributed EY quote (🟡).
**Illustrative only, never presented as measured:** any attendee→buyer ratio (e.g. "a few dozen out of 3,000"). Say "a few dozen", "a handful", "maybe 20" — never a computed percentage as fact.

## Cost of Inaction

**Compounding cost.** The booth, travel and team days are already spent. Every day closer to the event without a shortlist narrows what can be done with it: after about one week before the doors open, there's no time to deliver or to open conversations.

**Trigger window.** A confirmed event 2–8 weeks out. This is the only real urgency. **Without an upcoming event there is no compelling event — don't manufacture one.** Route those prospects to the next season (Walk the Floor is the exception, with a longer cycle).

**Quantification — framework the buyer fills in (needs no clearance):**
> "What did the booth, travel and the team's two days cost you in total? Divide that by the number of conversations last year that actually turned into a sales opportunity. That's your cost per real conversation. Now compare it to $100 a match."

## Common Objections

### "What does this do that my team couldn't?" ⭐ the one that kills deals
**What's really being asked:** Is this worth paying for, or am I buying something I could do myself for free?
**Frequency / stage:** Post-delivery and at renewal — Cyberimpact, verbatim. Also pre-sale with teams that have a Director of Growth or SDRs.
**Response:**
> Your team could do it. The question is whether they will — in the two weeks before the event, across thousands of records, against your real buying criteria rather than the forty logos they already recognise. That's the work, and it's the half that never gets done. We don't replace your rep; we hand them the names and the reason each one is on the list. They still send the note and own the relationship.
**Do not:** Say the team can't do it. Pitch to companies with a strong internal SDR capability and a low-ACV product — they are a disqualifier, not an objection to overcome.
**Proof to reach for:** Gestion Vigie (unattributed) — no internal prep stack, recommendations included existing clients.

### "$1,500 is a lot."
**What's really being asked:** Will this pay for itself?
**Frequency / stage:** Proposal stage, smaller companies.
**Response:**
> It's $100 a match, and you only pay for matches that actually exist at this event. For most of our sponsors, one conversation that turns into a deal pays for it many times over. What did the booth and the two days cost you? Divide that by the real opportunities you got last year.
**Do not:** Discount below $750. Promise "no results, no charge." Sell 15 when the event holds 10.
**Proof to reach for:** The buyer's own cost-per-conversation framework.

### "Can you just book the meetings for us?"
**What's really being asked:** I want the outcome, not the preparation.
**Frequency / stage:** After first delivery — Axeptio.
**Response:**
> We don't, on purpose. The meeting goes better when it comes from you, and the relationship stays yours — nobody from outside is in the conversation with your prospect. What we do is make that first message easy: each name comes with the reason it's on the list and an opening draft.
**Do not:** Say yes. Imply it's on the roadmap. It is a different business model at roughly four times the price.
**Proof to reach for:** None needed — it's a stance.

### "We already have the event app / Swapcard / Grip."
**What's really being asked:** Isn't this a duplicate?
**Frequency / stage:** Discovery.
**Response:**
> Keep it — it's useful for scheduling and scanning. It matches people on the tags they picked themselves. We start from your buying criteria and outside signals, and we work only for you. It's a layer on top, not a replacement.
**Do not:** Name competitors in cold outreach. Criticise the organizer's app — the organizer is our partner.
**Proof to reach for:** HOP! quote.

### "We can get the attendee list ourselves."
**What's really being asked:** Why pay for something I can have for free?
**Frequency / stage:** Discovery, teams with organizer access.
**Response:**
> Often you can. What you get is a name, a company and a title. What we add is the four dimensions — role, tech stack, buying signal — and the cut from thousands to the handful worth your time, with a reason on each.
**Do not:** Claim exclusivity over the list.
**Proof to reach for:** Mechanism, not a number.

### "What if there aren't 15 good matches?"
**What's really being asked:** Will I pay for filler?
**Frequency / stage:** Proposal, narrow ICPs — the EY case.
**Response:**
> Then we tell you, and you pay for what exists. We'd rather deliver ten real matches than fifteen with five that don't fit.
**Do not:** Promise a fixed number. Pro-rate after the fact without saying so up front.
**Proof to reach for:** Per-match pricing.

### "The event's in 5 days." / "Not for this event."
**What's really being asked:** Is it too late, or not a priority?
**Frequency / stage:** Late inbound, or low urgency.
**Response:**
> We need about a week to deliver and you need time to open conversations, so this one's probably too close. Which event is next on your calendar? Let's set it up for that one now.
**Do not:** Rush a delivery that will be weak. Invent urgency when there's no event.
**Proof to reach for:** —

### "You're a small company."
**What's really being asked:** Will you deliver, and will you still be here?
**Frequency / stage:** Mid-market and larger buyers.
**Response:**
> We are, and that's why the deliverable is narrow and the price is per unit. There's no platform to integrate, no procurement, no annual commitment. You share an ICP and pay for the matches we actually find.
**Do not:** Inflate team size or claim scale.
**Proof to reach for:** 11 clients across 6 events; one repeat client.

### "How do you handle attendee data? (Loi 25)"
**What's really being asked:** Will this create a privacy or compliance problem for us?
**Frequency / stage:** Companies over ~100 employees, legal/RevOps.
**Response:**
> We work from the organizer's list under agreement, and it's used only for this event's shortlist, within the terms the organizer sets. Consent stays with the organizer's opt-in and opt-out. We can send you the data handling terms before you sign.
**Do not:** Improvise retention or deletion terms that aren't in the contract.
**Proof to reach for:** Data handling one-pager (🔴 still to produce — see Blocked).

### "We don't really know our ICP precisely."
**What's really being asked:** (readiness) Am I ready for this?
**Frequency / stage:** Discovery, early-stage sponsors.
**Response:**
> That's what the ICP brief is for — twenty minutes. We'll ask who your last three good clients were and build from there. The sharper it is, the shorter and better the shortlist.
**Do not:** Accept "everybody with a website is our client" — that's the low-ACV, broad-targeting pattern that fails.
**Proof to reach for:** —

### "The opening drafts sound like AI."
**What's really being asked:** I don't want to send something generic.
**Frequency / stage:** Post-delivery.
**Response:**
> Fair — use them as a starting point, not a script. Send us one or two messages you've written that worked and we'll draft in your voice. The part that matters most is the signal on each name; that's what makes the note specific.
**Do not:** Defend the drafts as finished copy.
**Proof to reach for:** —

### Organizer — "We can't share our attendee list."
**What's really being asked:** Is this a data or trust risk for us?
**Response:**
> Everything is governed by contract: the list is used only for your event, within the terms you define, never resold or reused. Your existing opt-in and opt-out govern the program. You share a list; we return value to your sponsors.
**Do not:** Minimise the concern.
**Proof to reach for:** Existing agreements with CQCD, Groupe Contex, ALL IN.

### Organizer — "Our sponsors won't pay for another add-on."
**What's really being asked:** Will this annoy my sponsors?
**Response:**
> The ones who come for business, not visibility, are looking for exactly this. You can offer it as an option or bundle it into your top tier — HOP! did both.
**Do not:** Promise sponsor uptake numbers.
**Proof to reach for:** HOP! (confirm before naming).

### Organizer — "We already have a matchmaking app."
**Response:**
> Keep it. The app matches attendees who opt in on shared tags; we prepare each sponsor with a shortlist against their own buying criteria. It makes your app more used, not less.
**Do not:** Criticise their app or vendor.

### Organizer — "Why should sponsor ROI be our problem?"
**What's really being asked:** (pain awareness) I don't feel this pain.
**Response:**
> Because it's your renewal rate. Across trade shows, about 38% of exhibitors don't come back the next year. Sponsors who do business at your event are the easiest renewals you'll ever have.
**Do not:** Push if the organizer genuinely has no retention problem — qualify out.
**Proof to reach for:** CEIR 62% retention (cleared).
