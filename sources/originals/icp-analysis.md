# ICP Analysis: BETA Labs
**Based on:** CRM export (11 clients, 4 prospects), Sales Process doc (3 detailed deal timelines), Company Profile, GTM Playbook, GTM Intelligence Report
**Analysis date:** 2026-03-11

---

## ICP Sweet Spot

**B2B SaaS or Marketing/Tech services company, 20–150 employees, confirmed sponsor or exhibitor at an upcoming Quebec or Ontario B2B conference within 2–8 weeks, with a GM, VP Marketing, or BDM as the direct buyer — motivated to generate pipeline, not brand visibility.**

The urgency of a specific upcoming event is the single most important deal catalyst. Remove the event context and the sales cycle triples. The buyer must have direct budget authority (not "Event" manager alone). Marketing as an industry is the strongest vertical signal: these buyers already think in pipeline terms and close fastest.

---

## Critical Correction to Company Context

The previous company-context.md stated: *"no confirmed paying customers"* and framed event organizers as the primary customer. **Both are wrong.**

The CRM shows 11 paying clients across 6 events. The actual dynamic is:

- **Paying customer = the sponsor/exhibitor company** (always)
- **Event organizer = lead generation channel** (not the buyer)

The organizer partnership model works as follows: organizer sends an email to their sponsor list referencing BETA Labs → sponsor books a meeting → BETA Labs closes directly. The organizer is never the paying party — they are an introduction mechanism that dramatically accelerates the sales cycle (Mirego and Expedibox both closed in 1–15 days via organizer intro vs. slower direct cycles for Walk the Floor accounts).

## Firmographic Filter Recommendations

- **Industry:** Marketing SaaS, Marketing Services, B2B Tech/SaaS, Tech Services (Development) — strongest; Retail, Construction Tech — viable via organizer channel; Government, Enterprise Professional Services — avoid or deprioritize
- **Employee count:** 20–200 (sweet spot 30–150). Above 400: enterprise procurement kills speed. Below 15: budget risk.
- **Revenue range:** Not captured in CRM, but deal size signals suggest companies with $1M–$20M ARR range
- **Geography:** Quebec-first (all 2025 deals); Ontario emerging (Social Next Ottawa 26: Cyberimpact, Digital Revamp); rest of Canada — no data yet
- **Product type:** Product (SaaS) = 5 clients; Service = 5 clients — split is even, product type is not a differentiator
- **Attendee status:** Confirmed sponsor or exhibitor strongly preferred over Walk the Floor. Sponsors have already committed budget to the event → easiest to justify the add-on

## Buying Committee Structure

**Champion (daily pain carrier):**
- Title: SDR Manager, Director of Growth, VP of Customer Success, BDM
- Pain: Attends events, returns with a pile of badge scans, no pipeline to show for it. Accountable for event ROI.
- Observed: Cyberimpact (SDR/Director of Growth), Mirego (VP Customer Success), Expedibox (BDM)

**Economic Buyer (makes the GO decision):**
- Title: GM, VP Marketing, CEO, BDM/Partner
- Pain: Justifying the event spend to leadership. Wants a number (meetings) not a feeling (brand presence).
- Observed: Cyberimpact (GM), Mirego (VP Marketing), Nexus (CEO), EY (BDM/Partner)
- Key insight: In companies under 50 employees, GM = both champion and economic buyer → single-threaded deal, fastest close.

**Gatekeeper (slows or blocks):**
- Title: Event Manager, Legal/Procurement (enterprise)
- Risk: "Event" title buyers without budget mandate can champion but not close. EY's complexity likely reflects a multi-stakeholder procurement path.
- Observed: Autodesk, Premier, Gouvernement du Québec all have "Event" as buyer — these came through organizer channel, suggesting organizer made the intro directly to event managers who then escalated.

**Loi 25 / Data Privacy gatekeeper:**
- Not explicitly seen in CRM but called out in sales doc as an active objection — RevOps/Legal at larger companies will raise this. Prepare a one-pager.

## Win Rate Analysis

All 11 CRM rows are marked "Client" status — 100% close rate on recorded deals. This is misleading (likely only closed deals were entered). However, the sales process reveals meaningful cycle-length variance:

| Segment | Days to GO | Days to Deposit | Deal Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organizer-introduced sponsor (small co.) | 1 day | 1–2 days | $1,500 | Best pattern: Mirego, Cyberimpact, Digital Revamp |
| Organizer-introduced sponsor (mid co.) | 10 days | 15 days | $750–$1,500 | Expedibox — some negotiation |
| Direct outbound, Walk the Floor | 7–21 days | 78–84 days | $750–$4,000 | CDWare, Nexus — good ACV, slow payment |
| Enterprise sponsor | 162+ days | No deposit yet | $1,500 | EY — avoid as primary target |

**Best-ROI motion:** Organizer introduces BETA Labs to sponsor list → sponsor is already event-committed → closes in 1–2 days. This is the motion to replicate at scale.

## Wrong-Fit Signals

- **Enterprise (500+ employees):** EY at 400K employees — 162 days to GO, no deposit. Procurement complexity destroys the urgency that makes BETA Labs' model work.
- **Government:** Gouvernement du Québec is a client but likely came through Batimatech organizer channel. Government procurement cycles and data privacy rules (beyond Loi 25) make direct outreach risky.
- **"Event" manager as sole buyer:** When the only contact is an event manager without budget authority, the deal stalls waiting for escalation. Needs a GM or VP to co-own the decision.
- **Walk the Floor without a specific event:** CDWare (N/A event) has $4K ACV but 78 days to deposit. No event urgency = no natural close date = slow. Viable but not the primary motion.
- **No confirmed upcoming event:** Prospects without a specific event in the next 8 weeks have no urgency lever. Don't prioritize.

## ICP Tier Definitions

### Tier 1 — Ideal

All of the following:
- Company type: B2B SaaS or B2B services, 20–200 employees
- Industry: Marketing, MarTech, Sales Tech, B2B Software, or Digital Agency
- Role of buyer: GM, VP Marketing, Director of Marketing, or BDM with direct budget authority
- Situation: Confirmed sponsor or exhibitor at a specific upcoming B2B conference in Quebec or Ontario
- Time to event: 2–8 weeks (urgency window is active)
- Pain signal: Company has attended events before without a pre-meeting strategy (measuring success by badge count or booth traffic)
- Source: Organizer-introduced OR founder direct outbound with event-specific hook

*Archetype: Cyberimpact, Mirego, Digital Revamp*

### Tier 2 — Strong

Most of the following:
- Company type: B2B SaaS or professional services, 150–400 employees
- Industry: Tech Services, Construction Tech, Retail, or other B2B verticals
- Role: Marketing Director, BDM, Partnership Manager, or Sales Director
- Situation: Sponsor/exhibitor at an upcoming event OR Walk the Floor with a clear commercial objective and a named list of targets
- Time to event: Up to 12 weeks
- Pain signal: Has done events but can't attribute pipeline to them; sales team frustrated with event follow-up quality

*Archetype: Expedibox, Nexus, Autodesk (via organizer), CDWare (Walk the Floor)*

### Tier 3 — Possible (Qualify Carefully)

- Enterprise (400+ employees): expect 3–6 month sales cycle, multi-stakeholder approval, legal review. Only pursue if organizer relationship is already established.
- Government: Avoid direct outbound; only through organizer partner.
- Company with no confirmed upcoming event: Engage but do not prioritize. Flag for future event season.
- "Event" title only: Engage to qualify, but require a GM/VP intro before advancing.

*Archetype: EY, Gouvernement du Québec*

## Event-Based Segmentation (Secondary ICP Dimension)

BETA Labs' ICP is inseparable from the event calendar. The right company at the wrong time (no upcoming event) is a Tier 3. The right company with an event in 4 weeks is Tier 1.

**High-value event types to target for organizer partnerships:**
- Quebec/Ontario B2B tech conferences: ALL IN, Startupfest, RDV Numérique — already in portfolio
- Sector summits with heavy sponsor/exhibitor presence: HOP! (retail), Grand Batimatech (construction), Social Next (marketing/social)
- Events with 200–2,000 attendees and meaningful sponsor tiers ($5K–$50K packages) — right size for BETA Labs' model

**Event types to avoid or deprioritize:**
- Mega-conferences (10,000+ attendees): attendee list too large to qualify manually, organizer procurement complex
- Community/meetup events: too small, no sponsor budget
- Consumer events: wrong audience entirely

## Strategic Notes

**1. The organizer channel is the growth engine — invest in it.**
The fastest and easiest deals in the CRM all started with an organizer email to their sponsor list. BETA Labs should prioritize signing 5–10 recurring event partnerships in Quebec and Ontario before pursuing direct outbound at scale. Each organizer partnership unlocks a pre-warmed list of sponsors ready to receive BETA Labs' pitch.

**2. Marketing sector is the highest-density vertical.**
Cyberimpact, Axeptio, Digital Revamp (clients) + Glowtify, HighLevel (prospects) all sit in the marketing/MarTech space. These buyers understand pipeline metrics, move fast, and are natural champions. A vertical-specific angle ("for MarTech sponsors who attend marketing events") could anchor outbound messaging.

**3. CDWare is a signal worth following.**
CDWare bought a $4,000 package with no event attached — a standalone ICP matchmaking service. This is a different, higher-value motion (proactive business development, not event-tied). The longer cycle is a real cost, but the ACV is 2.5–5x higher. This "always-on matchmaking" product could be a Tier 2 offering for companies actively hiring or prospecting between events.

**4. The $1,500 / 15 matches package is the standard — protect it.**
8 of 11 clients are at the $1,500 price point. Expedibox and Nexus negotiated down to $750. Avoid downward price pressure by anchoring to the ROI math: a single won deal from a qualified meeting ($10K–$100K ACV for most clients) pays for the package 7–67x over.

**5. Social Next Ottawa = first Ontario foothold.**
Two clients from Social Next Ottawa 26 (Cyberimpact, Digital Revamp). This is the proof point for geographic expansion. The organizer of Social Next is a key relationship to deepen.

**6. Loi 25 is a procurement gate, not a dealbreaker.**
Prepare a one-page data handling summary (how attendee lists are handled, stored, deleted) to pre-empt the objection at larger companies. Small companies (under 50 employees) won't raise it. Mid-market and enterprise will.
