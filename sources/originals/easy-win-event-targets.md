## Scoring Criteria: What Makes an Event "Easy"

An easy win for BETA Labs means:

| Criteria | Easy Win | Hard Sell |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Attendees** | 5,000-30,000 (sweet spot) | <1,000 or >100,000 |
| **Cross-industry dynamic** | Tech vendors → non-tech buyers | Vendor-to-vendor (everyone is tech) |
| **Existing matchmaking** | None or basic badge scanning | Swapcard/Grip embedded, hosted buyer programs |
| **Organizer accessibility** | Independent or mid-tier Informa/SME | Fortress (GSMA, Deutsche Messe, CTA) |
| **Sponsor sophistication** | No internal GTM tools, new to the event | Has SDR team, uses Clay/Sales Nav already |
| **Organizer pain awareness** | Knows sponsors are struggling, wants retention | Doesn't think about sponsor ROI |
| **Frequency** | Annual (recurring revenue) | Biennial or one-off |
| **Travel from Montreal** | <2hr flight, same timezone | >5hr flight, big timezone gap |

## STAY AWAY: Events That Are Too Hard

### ❌ CES — Las Vegas
- **142,000 attendees, 4,500 exhibitors.** Scale overwhelms BETA Labs' current tech.
- **Vendelux is deeply embedded.** They publish CES event guides and actively sell pre-event meetings to CES exhibitors.
- **Organizer (CTA) is a fortress.** Getting attendee list access at CES would require significant credibility.
- **$1,500 pricing is laughable.** Even $5K is noise to a CES sponsor spending $200K+ on their booth. Pricing strategy not ready.
- **Wait until BETA Labs has 5+ US case studies and scaled tech.**

### ❌ HIMSS — Las Vegas
- **25,000-28,000 attendees, 950+ exhibitors.** Good size, but health IT is heavily regulated.
- **Data privacy concerns extreme.** Health IT attendee data may fall under HIPAA-adjacent regulations.
- **Swapcard/Grip likely embedded.** Large health events tend to use event platforms.
- **Better as Event #3 or #4 in Vegas**, after Channel Partners and NAB prove the model.

### ❌ ShopTalk — Las Vegas
- **Has its own hosted buyer program ("Meetup").** This is a direct substitute for what BETA Labs offers. The organizer has already built the matchmaking into the event. There is no gap to fill.
- **Competing with the organizer's own product is a losing battle.**

### ❌ Dreamforce — San Francisco
- **Salesforce's own conference.** Single-vendor ecosystem. Attendees are Salesforce users, sponsors are Salesforce ISV partners. Everyone is tech-selling-to-tech.
- **Audience is ultra-sophisticated.** They use Clay, Sales Nav, ZoomInfo, and Vendelux already.
- **"What does this do that we can't do ourselves?"** — Cyberimpact objection at scale.

### ❌ RSA Conference — San Francisco
- **45,000+ attendees, 600+ exhibitors.** Good fit on paper (cybersecurity vendors selling to CISOs across all industries).
- **But: Vendelux's home turf.** SF Bay Area is Vendelux HQ. RSA sponsors are exactly Vendelux's target customer (500+ employee tech companies with event marketing teams).
- **Highest competition density of any event.** Save for later.

### ❌ Social Next Toronto
- **700 attendees.** Below BETA Labs' 1,000-attendee hard floor.
- **Elizabeth already confirmed this lesson** from the Social Next experience with Cyberimpact.

### ❌ DX3 (Retail & Marketing) — Toronto
- **2,000 attendees.** Just above the floor, but the event is pivoting away from trade show format toward "intimate, strategy-driven experience." Smaller exhibit floor. Hard to sell matchmaking when the event is designed for everyone to meet organically.
- **Marketing/SaaS audience = sophisticated buyers.** Same "we can do this ourselves" objection risk.

### ❌ Automate — Detroit
- **30,000+ attendees, 700 exhibitors.** Great fit, but it's biennial (next: 2027, then 2029). Can't build recurring annual revenue. Use Battery Show (annual) instead and add Automate as a bonus in the right years.
