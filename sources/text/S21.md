# 2. Las Vegas: The Big Bet

## 2.1 Opportunity Analysis

Las Vegas is the single highest-value expansion target for BETA Labs. The city hosts 8+ qualifying B2B events annually, each with thousands of sponsors who desperately need pre-event meetings. The sponsor pain is acute and well-documented.

### The Pain is Real and Quantified

Average cost per lead at a Vegas show: $1,200 (vs. $400 at regional shows). Sponsors are paying 3x more per lead and getting worse quality.

A 10x20 booth at a major Vegas show costs $25,000-$55,000 in total investment once travel, show services, and freight are factored in. That is 40-60% more than the exhibit and space cost alone.

Only 37% of exhibitors formally measure trade show ROI after each event. The rest are flying blind. BETA Labs provides measurable, pre-booked meetings that directly address this gap.

Badge scanning produces 70-90% unqualified contacts: press, competitors, students, and casual browsers dilute the lead pool. BETA Labs' enriched hit list eliminates this waste entirely.

Despite this pain, 42% of exhibitors increased their trade show budgets in 2025/2026 vs. the prior year. The money is there. The problem is ROI, not budget.

### Vendelux's Blind Spot: The Organizer Channel

Vendelux sells directly to sponsors via their SaaS platform. They have no organizer partnership model. Their pricing ($20K+/year) targets companies with dedicated event marketing teams. This leaves two massive gaps:

Mid-market sponsors (20-200 employees) who can't justify a $20K annual platform for 2-3 events per year. BETA Labs' $1,500-$4,000 per-event pricing is perfect for this segment.

The organizer channel: Vendelux doesn't sell through organizers. BETA Labs' event-organizer-as-distributor model means the organizer does the selling, bundles BETA Labs into sponsorship packages, and earns a revenue share. This is a fundamentally different go-to-market.

# 3. Detroit: The Smart First Move

## 3.1 Opportunity Analysis

Detroit is the lowest-friction expansion market for BETA Labs. Same timezone, 1.5-hour flight (or 8-hour drive), no language barrier, and a massive cross-industry dynamic that perfectly mirrors BETA Labs' sweet spot: tech vendors selling to non-tech industrial buyers.

### Why Detroit is Unique

The automotive industry is in the middle of its biggest disruption since the assembly line. The EV transition, software-defined vehicles, battery supply chains, and autonomous driving have created an entire ecosystem of new technology vendors trying to sell to traditional OEMs and Tier 1 suppliers. These new vendors don't have existing relationships. They can't walk the floor at Battery Show and know who to talk to. They are BETA Labs' perfect customer.

Cross-industry tension is exactly right: EV tech, battery tech, automation, AI, and software companies selling to automotive OEMs, Tier 1 suppliers, plant managers, and procurement directors. This is the HOP!/Batimatech dynamic at 10-50x scale.

Sponsor pain is high and specific: An automation company paying $30K+ for a Fabtech booth needs to meet the right plant managers and procurement directors out of 30,000 attendees. They cannot do it by walking the floor.

New entrants = highest BETA Labs value: Companies entering the automotive supply chain for the first time (battery tech, charging infrastructure, AI/ML for manufacturing) have zero existing relationships. BETA Labs' intelligence-driven matchmaking is their only option besides cold walking.

# 4. Toronto: The Validation Market

## 4.1 Why Toronto Now

Toronto was not in the original 8-city research, but it should have been. It is the single most logical first expansion market for BETA Labs, for one simple reason: it eliminates every variable except the product-market fit question.

Same country: No US business registration, no cross-border tax complexity, no visa requirements. Invoice in CAD.

Same timezone (EST): No coordination challenges. Sales calls, event attendance, and follow-up all happen in real time.

1-hour flight or 5.5-hour drive from Montreal: Lower travel cost than any other market. Elizabeth could attend an event and be home the same day.

Larger, more impersonal business culture than Quebec: The 'kingmaker' dynamic and 'village' effect that suppress BETA Labs' value in Quebec don't exist in Toronto. People don't already know each other.

Elizabeth already has a Toronto data point: Cyberimpact attended Social Next Toronto. This is a warm thread to pull.

57+ trade shows per year: Toronto is Canada's largest event city with major convention infrastructure.

### Strategic Role: De-Risk Before the US

Toronto is not a revenue maximizer. It is a validation market. The purpose is to prove that BETA Labs' model works in English, at larger event scales, in a more impersonal business culture, before investing in US entry. If it works in Toronto, the Detroit and Vegas playbooks become dramatically lower-risk.

The event matchmaking market is evolving rapidly. Swapcard launched unified registration in April 2026. Eventdex is positioning as the enterprise matchmaking leader. Vendelux is growing at 136% YoY. But the market remains fragmented by model (platform vs. managed service) and buyer (organizer vs. sponsor).

| Business Matchmaking Platforms | Eventdex, b2match | Organizers of B2B forums | LOW: brokerage model, not concierge |

BETA Labs occupies a unique position: the only pure concierge service that enriches actual attendee lists with external data and delivers confirmed pre-booked meetings to mid-market sponsors. Vendelux is the closest competitor, but their SaaS model, enterprise pricing ($20K+/year), and predictive (not actual) attendee data create meaningful differentiation.

### Hosted Buyer Programs: Evolving Threat

The hosted buyer model is shifting. IBTM World introduced a paid 'Visitor Buyer' option in 2025, moving away from obligation-heavy free attendance. Swapcard powered PCMA's hosted buyer program at Convening Leaders 2026, connecting $3M-$5M budget buyers with suppliers. This suggests organizers are professionalizing their matchmaking offerings.

Implication for BETA Labs: As organizers invest more in hosted buyer programs, they may resist third-party matchmaking services. BETA Labs' counter-argument: hosted buyer programs serve the top 5% of sponsors. BETA Labs serves the other 95%.

## 5.2 BETA Labs' Defensible Moat

BETA Labs has four defensible advantages that no competitor replicates:

Actual attendee data: Organizer shares the real attendee list under NDA. Vendelux predicts; BETA Labs knows.

External enrichment: Overlays LinkedIn, Crunchbase, BuiltWith, and funding data on top of the attendee list. No platform does this at the individual attendee level.

Pay-per-meeting pricing: No annual SaaS commitment. $100/meeting or fixed packages. Accessible to mid-market sponsors.

Organizer distribution channel: Revenue-share model incentivizes organizers to sell BETA Labs into their sponsorship packages. No competitor has this channel.

# 6. Pricing Strategy by Market

## 6.1 Why Quebec Pricing Breaks in New Markets

At Quebec events with $3K-$5K booth costs, $1,500 for 15 matches is 30-50% of the sponsor's total event investment. This creates scrutiny. At international events with $40K-$100K+ booth costs, $1,500 is a rounding error too cheap to take seriously. Organizers won't bother reselling it.

| Toronto | $5K-$15K | $2,000-$3,000 | 12-18 | $140-$200 | Moderate step up. PDAC/Fabtech sponsors invest more than QC. |

## 6.3 Dynamic Pricing Opportunity

Elizabeth is already exploring flexible/dynamic pricing (charge for how many good matches actually exist, not a fixed number). This is even more important in new markets:

At PDAC (32K attendees, 1,300 exhibitors): There might be 40+ great matches for a mining tech sponsor. Upsell to 25-30 matches at $200/match = $5,000-$6,000.

At a niche Detroit event (8K attendees): There might only be 8-10 great matches. Charge $2,400-$3,000 for 8-10 matches instead of padding to 15.

Dynamic pricing eliminates the EY problem: No more selling 15 matches and only delivering 10. Sell what you can actually deliver.
